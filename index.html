A linear distribution feeder

vₜ = 1 − Rᵥpₜ − Xᵥqₜload + XᵥEqₜinv

This declared 24 bus radial feeder uses balanced LinDistFlow, with bus 1 fixed at 1 pu. Voltage magnitudes follow net real demand, reactive demand and inverter injection through electrical line impedances. The approximation neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. It is a constructed feeder, not a measured utility network.

The bases are 12.47 kV and 1 MVA. Line resistances span 0.45 to 0.90 Ω and reactances span 0.30 to 0.46 Ω. Homes consume 70 kW at power factor 0.95. Battery buses consume 50 kW, and solar buses generate 80 kW. Eight 120 kVA inverters accept reactive commands up to ±70 kvar.

qₜ₊₁inv = a qₜinv + (1 − a)uₜ   ·   a = exp(−1/2)

One sample represents one second. Inverters have a two second response time. The fixed reference v̄ is the nominal feeder profile with zero inverter reactive injection. The goal is to restore that profile after power changes, with a penalty for reactive dispatch. Actual voltage is shown in pu without clipping.

cₜ = 35‖vₜ − v̄‖² + 0.08‖uₜ‖²

Reactive commands u use the 1 MVA base. Clicks increase demand, reduce available solar generation or increase battery charging. Repeated clicks add requested power changes. Solar loss and battery charging respect their available power limits. A click changes the next voltage sample, whose state cost appears at that sample.

The voltage approximation follows LinDistFlow voltage regulation. It omits unbalanced phases, protection, frequency dynamics and nonlinear inverter controls.

What is being learned

A fixed LQR feedback gain regulates voltage deviations from v̄. A shared matrix G calibrates five power forecasts using the latest 60 revealed samples, starting at step 30. Raw power forecasts are sealed before interventions. All four controllers share the same physical disturbances. Future power is anchored to its current measurement, then adjusted by calibrated forecast changes. Feedback only holds the current power. This lets every controller respond to an unexpected load after observing it, without access to event metadata.

Forecast power → command reactive power → measure → fit G

The graph and matrix rank useful forecast sources for the eight inverter buses. These are communication recommendations. They do not switch feeder lines or restrict the deployed controller. Live data keeps changing, so learned gains and recommendations need not converge monotonically. Freeze data instead holds the observations fixed and shows fitting error relative to the exact ridge minimizer.

One method and three baselines

Adaptive fits a matrix. Scalar adaptation fits one common multiplier using the same objective and observations. Fixed prediction keeps its original gains. Feedback only uses current information without future forecasts. The three predictive controllers coincide until learning starts at step 30.

Reliable, uniformly corrupted and mixed reliability forecasts test different conditions. Clicked pulses are unexpected by all controllers and test recovery. They do not directly test the benefit of recognizing unreliable prediction channels. The comparison includes every sample and reports higher costs as well as lower ones.
