A distribution feeder under changing power

The experiment regulates a constructed, balanced radial feeder with 24 buses. A fixed substation supplies homes, solar generation and batteries. The target is the nominal voltage profile before power disturbances.

Disturbances and forecasts

Background net power follows a correlated process with a 6 kW scale. Forecasts are fixed when issued. Every controller receives the same physical disturbances and forecasts.

Communication topology is selected by default. Forecasts equal the resulting voltage forcing plus independent Gaussian errors. Noise level β is chosen from 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2, initially 1. Sources at bus multiples of three have error standard deviation 4β times their physical forcing scale. Other sources use 0.05β. These scales depend only on the feeder and background generator. Quality labels are not supplied to the learner.

In Forecast mode, clicking a bus corrupts or repairs its forecast source. Marked sources remain noisy until repaired. Only newly issued forecasts change. Inspecting a matrix cell exposes a separate Corrupt or Repair source button that targets its column source, not its inverter receiver, without starting playback. Saved topology history is read only, and this action is disabled at zero noise. Their noise uses the selected level above. The final source reliability map is retained when comparison starts. The learner sees observations rather than the source quality labels shown on screen.

In Power disturbance mode, clicking homes adds demand, solar reduces generation and batteries increase charging. Requests of 50, 100 or 150 kW add and then decay. Solar loss is limited by available generation, and battery charging is capped at 90 kW. Orange badges show actual applied kW. Continuous mode repeats requests independently at every active bus. Click several buses to attack them together, click one again to stop only that bus, or use Stop all. Pause stops repeated requests. Pulse mode adds one per click or repeats while held. These unexpected attacks are absent from forecasts and primarily test the common feedback.

The source inspector reports only the last revealed forecast and forcing pair, never a future observation. For action t, its raw forecast was issued at max(0, t − 4) seconds and the forcing is revealed at t + 1. Error is forecast minus revealed voltage forcing, in mpu where 1 mpu is 0.001 pu. This is an input forcing quantity, not bus voltage or pure source noise. Unexpected power changes contribute to the error. Changing reliability while paused cannot rewrite the displayed pair or sealed forecasts. Discovery uses dense messages regardless of candidate links. During comparison, a separate label states whether the source is permitted by the inspected receiver’s fixed support; this does not promise a nonzero coefficient or improve the forecast. Saved history hides current observations.

The command disclosure reconstructs the latest receiver action for all four controllers in kvar. It separates the inspected raw source, all other prediction messages, shared feedback, the unbounded request and the bounded command. Prediction terms sum every temporal block of the preview actually issued for that action. For matrix adaptation, raw source attribution uses the actual gain L C, not an intermediate corrected channel. The source gain norm is a visible column in pu command per pu forcing, accessible without hover. It is the Euclidean norm across the actual temporal gains for that raw source and does not depend on forecast amplitude. A zero source contribution is not evidence of an absent link. Keyboard matrix navigation and native disclosure activation preserve plant state, reliability and playback. Highlighted links and the support label refer to Learned sparse or Matrix adaptation, while other policies may use different sources. A permitted link can have zero gain in a particular row. These are additive terms before limiting, not causal shares of the limited command. No future realized forcing is used and this read only view changes no controller or cost.

The inspected source also reports raw forecast RMSE and signed mean error over at most the last 60 revealed pairs in the current phase. The window can contain multiple reliability settings and unforecast physical changes, so it does not identify pure source noise or prove improvement. The latest setting readout separates first issue time, forecast target and first revealed pair, with the number of window pairs issued under that setting. Reset seals the initial five messages, so a toggle at time 0 first affects the message issued at time 1 for target action 5, observed at time 6. At later time t, the first new target is t + 4. A new message may affect the current preview command immediately at issue, before its target is observed. Repeated paused toggles use the latest setting for future issuance, without rewriting messages or errors. The display resets its window for fresh comparison, hides current statistics in saved history and preserves version 13 replay. It never changes learning, feedback or the physical stream.

Bounded solar outage preset

Reference remains the default. The optional solar outage leaves discovery unchanged. In comparison actions 30 through 49, solar generation at buses 5, 7, 14 and 21 is set to zero, then restored at action 50. This represents sudden loss of solar active output while the grid connected inverters retain reactive support. It is not inverter disconnection or a utility measurement. Nominal lost generation is 320 kW. Actual lost generation follows the identical background stream and is bounded by the existing 90 kW generation limit per site, at most 360 kW total. Requested and applied power are displayed. Existing inverter limits, feedback, objective and prediction coefficients are unchanged.

The outage is an unannounced physical intervention and is absent from every issued forecast. It is not a forecast attack and does not promise to separate controller costs. All controllers face the same realized power and raw forecasts. If clicked attacks overlap, the scenario is applied first and manual pulses receive only the remaining feasible change, so applied power is not counted twice. Saved trials replay the schedule from their stored scenario option.

The paired scenario audit uses all seeds 1 through 20 at β 0, 1 and 2. It reports reference and outage costs, wins, ties, losses and unsuccessful recoveries. Recovery means the maximum voltage difference from the same controller in its matched no-outage trial is at most 0.0001 pu for five consecutive samples after restoration. The report gives the confirmation time, not the first entry. This counterfactual metric is separate from the live nominal-profile deviation and does not remove outage costs.

Chart markers use the immutable simulation event log. A forecast setting marker appears immediately at the actual toggle time, including while paused, and distinguishes marked noisy from marked quiet. Its first eligible issue, target and reveal times do not promise that a message changed, since later toggles before issuance can replace the setting. Issued forecasts stay fixed. Power markers identify bounded requests after execution, at the first affected state time, rather than claiming every requested kW was applied. Solar events, power requests and forecast changes have separate counts in mixed groups. These annotations change no controller, sample, cost or saved replay.

Comparison protocol

The phase badge distinguishes dense discovery from comparison. On comparison it reports the actual discovery sample count and confirms offline link selection and sparse controller synthesis. Automatic discovery uses 180 samples; manual comparison reports its actual episode length, which can differ from 180. Communication comparison fixes links and prediction coefficients. Prediction adaptation fixes links but continues correcting predictions. No joint online topology adaptation is implied. Reviewing topology history changes only the link view, not playback, the live plant, costs or deployed support. The header explicitly labels saved discovery links, and current source quality labels are suppressed there rather than attributed to the past. Latest or a matrix view change restores current links and source controls immediately, without stepping the plant.

One 180 sample discovery episode uses dense prediction messages. Comparison then resets states and costs on an independent disturbance stream. All four controllers share the feeder, limits, objective and disturbances.

Communication topology compares Learned sparse, Fixed sparse, Dense prediction and Feedback only. Each sparse policy receives its local source and three remote sources per inverter. Their coefficients and selected links stay fixed during comparison, isolating the effect of link selection. Sparse policies use 24 remote prediction links and dense uses 176. Eight local channels and common state feedback are excluded.

Changing source reliability during comparison tests the already deployed links in a new environment. Use Learn again to discover links for that environment. Power disturbances test a different mechanism. Without inverter saturation, an unforecast disturbance adds the same state and input response to all four fixed controllers. Its common cost can dominate their prediction differences. Full physical costs remain in every curve.

The complete benchmark reports all five noise levels over twenty paired trials. This feeder uses one discovery episode and three remote sources per receiver. The paper uses six episodes, a different coupled model and twelve remote sources. Its numerical results are not reproduced here.

The additional forecast stress levels β 4, 8 and 16 increase the same synthetic Gaussian errors without changing physical power, controllers or limits. They are outside the reference grid. The selected source caption shows configured error SD in mpu of voltage forcing, where 1 mpu is 0.001 pu. This is not an actual bus voltage deviation. Changing β starts a fresh trial, so earlier physical attack costs do not carry into the new comparison.

The paired forecast stress audit retains all twenty seeds and all four costs, with and without severe simultaneous power requests. With forecast stress alone at β 8, learned sparse mean cost is 27.44 percent below fixed sparse and 59.60 percent below dense prediction. Feedback only is cheaper on every seed. Large physical attacks can still dominate the totals. These additional stress tests do not reproduce the paper's numerical advantage.

Prediction adaptation is a separate option with reliable, uniformly corrupted and mixed forecasts. Matrix and scalar corrections continue learning during its comparison under fixed sparse links. Its costs combine adaptation and selection effects.

Voltage is in pu and inverter injection in kvar. Cost uses the bounded reactive command on the 1 MVA base. Live cumulative cost sums executed stage costs. A completed benchmark adds the terminal voltage error penalty. A disturbance changes voltage at the next sample and is charged then.

Now reports maxᵢ |vᵢ − v̄ᵢ| across all buses for each controller. Peak is the maximum of that quantity over executed state samples and the initial state in the current phase. Each bus uses its own nominal reference, not 1 pu or the illustrative band. Values are in mpu, with 1 mpu = 0.001 pu. Peak time and bus are available on inspection. Discovery peaks reset when the fresh comparison starts. Limited counts inverters whose last raw reactive command exceeded the shared ±70 kvar bound. These read only diagnostics do not change controls or cost, and Peak is not an attack only recovery time.

The 0.95 to 1.05 pu band is a reference, not an enforced constraint. Voltage and cost are never clipped. Playback speed changes viewing pace without changing the one second physical sample.

Matrix history records actual candidate links during discovery. Added links are green and removed links amber. Comparison uses fixed selected links. Green and amber then compare the learned topology with fixed sparse, while history retains actual discovery changes. The cost gap view subtracts the matching sparse baseline, while the table retains totals.

LinDistFlow neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. This experiment omits unbalanced phases, thermal line limits, protection, frequency dynamics and battery energy limits. It is a constructed environment rather than a measured utility case.
