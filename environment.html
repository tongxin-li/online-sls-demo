Controlling a changing feeder

A choice of constructed radial feeders supplies homes, solar generation and batteries. Fresh trials start with quiet forecast sources and ordinary background power variation. Users introduce every power attack and forecast corruption themselves. It is an illustrative physical scenario, not measured utility data or a numerical reproduction of the paper.

Network sizes

The Network menu starts a fresh trial on the chosen feeder. Every size uses the same physical equations, equipment ratings, cost weights and learning rules. Counts and results belong to the selected network. Absolute cost sums over its buses and inverters, so costs from different sizes are not directly comparable.

User controlled environment

No scheduled demand surge, solar outage or forecast corruption runs in a fresh trial. The unchanged correlated power background has a 6 kW scale. Forecasts describe this background. Bus 6 starts selected and represents aggregate household demand. Restart demo and Network selection start a fresh paused trial with no attacks. Saved trials retain their recorded environment until an explicit new trial.

A five sample preview includes the current action. The forcing for target action t is sealed at max(0, t − 4) and observed at state t + 1. Previously issued forecasts never change. Manual power attacks are not included in the preview. All controllers receive the same realized physical power. The preview is a declared information assumption, not an implemented commercial forecasting service.

Homes increase aggregate demand, solar generation decreases and batteries increase charging. The toy has shorter electrical paths, so it offers a larger explicit demand stress. The 900 kW request is an illustrative severe aggregate event, not a typical household load. Solar loss remains limited by available generation, nominally 80 kW, and battery charging remains capped at 90 kW. These equipment limits can make an attack smaller than the request. Orange badges report actual applied kW.

Continuous mode maintains the selected additional request at each active bus. Click again to stop that bus or use Stop all. Pulse mode creates decaying requests and holding repeats them. Combined manual requests share the per bus cap above, even when pulses overlap. Stopping allows the residual disturbance to decay. Pause stops new requests and physical time. The background is separate from the manual attack cap.

All inverter commands remain bounded at ±70 kvar. No line impedance, feedback gain, cost weight or controller definition changes with the attack setting. All recovery costs stay in the trial. Large unforecast physical attacks can dominate shared recovery costs and narrow the relative differences between controllers.

Forecast corruption

Every source starts quiet. Clicking a bus in Forecast corruption mode makes its future messages noisy until that source is repaired. Its label is not supplied to the learner. A message estimates voltage forcing at its bus, which depends on power throughout the feeder. It is not simply a forecast of that bus’s own load.

Gaussian forecast error has standard deviation β times a fixed physical forcing scale times a source coefficient. Quiet sources use 0.05. A user corrupted source uses 32 on the toy and 8 on the larger feeders. The toy coefficient is four times the previous Strong setting. The default β is 4. The physical scale remains based on the original 6 kW background. The displayed noise in mpu describes forecast forcing error rather than an actual voltage drop.

Corruption changes only newly issued messages. Earlier forecasts remain sealed. Quiet sources provide useful alternatives, while corrupting every source removes them. Forecast corruption does not directly reduce generation or increase demand. More error can favor feedback only, and stronger attacks do not guarantee any method the lowest cost.

Archived operating profiles

Saved Standard and Strong trials retain their original schedules and initial source maps. Standard uses a 60 second cycle with household power changes of ±35 kW, solar losses of 0 to 30 kW and battery changes of ±15 kW. Strong adds a 30 second surge with a two second rise, four second hold and two second fall. Its peak changes are 120 kW for households and 60 kW for solar loss or battery charging, with the second radial branch delayed by four seconds. These known profiles enter their original previews and do not run in fresh user controlled trials.

Archived noisy source coefficients are 4 for Standard and 8 for Strong. Their default household sources begin corrupted. The archived stress characterization retains the original cycle, the exploratory sustained excursion and the selected short surge, including unfavorable outcomes. Those results describe their recorded environments rather than the current manual default.

Ongoing adaptive protocol

Fresh trials continuously update the full predictive state and input response matrices. Learning uses completed response windows only, with five samples of preview, a window of 60 completed rows and 30 completed rows before the first update at action 39. Every later action updates the response. Current incoming prediction links are reconsidered every 30 updates. Voltage, time and every accumulated stage cost remain continuous.

Newly issued forecasts retain their admitted links and full response matrices until their finite memory expires. A removed link stops sending new forecasts immediately. Its previously received values can still affect the command for a few samples. The interface separates current incoming links from these retiring cached contributions.

The sparse incoming budgets remain 6, 24 and 48 remote links for the three network sizes. Full response learning uses a central collection of revealed forecast and forcing pairs, and broadcasts new response parameters. That learning traffic is reported separately. Sparse actuator links alone are not a claim of lower total communication.

Fixed sparse retains its initial support and response. Dense prediction retains its full support and response. Feedback only uses the same stabilizing feedback without predictions. All four receive identical physical disturbances and use the same objective and limits. Neither startup costs nor unfavorable outcomes are removed.

This is an experimental shared response specialization with online topology reselection. It is not a literal execution of the manuscript’s independent response row algorithm and does not inherit a convergence guarantee for the changing saturated controller. The implementation and validation details are in the response implementation record.

Saved earlier trials restore their original controller and all costs exactly. Start adaptive demo on a saved source correction trial starts a fresh paused trial with continuous response and topology learning. Restart demo and Network selection also start fresh paused trials. There is one public demo and no version menu.

The following finite discovery and comparison lifecycle and recorded results belong to archived runs. They cannot supply performance percentages for the ongoing protocol. Playback pace depends on network size and rendering load while each physical sample always represents one second. Earlier slow 47 bus outcomes are retained in the linked engineering evidence. Retaining all samples through more than 1800 actions is finite evidence, not an indefinite memory guarantee. Exact validation and publication status remains in the repository progress record.

Archived fresh comparison

In the archived finite protocol, Run collects 180 discovery samples, then starts an independent 180 sample comparison with fresh plant states and costs. Its original guided scenario pauses at the comparison endpoint. Continue or Step extends that archived trial. Current Restart demo and Start manual trial leave this historical protocol and begin a clean paused manual trial. They do not replay a preset attack schedule.

Learned sparse, Fixed sparse, Dense prediction and Feedback only share the plant, feedback law, realized disturbances, objective and limits. Each sparse controller retains its local source and the network specific remote budget shown above. The reference uses 24 sparse remote links and 176 dense remote links. During comparison, selected links and prediction coefficients are fixed. Candidate changes during discovery and deployed connectivity are labeled separately.

The 24 bus complete scenario audit retains the exploratory grid, all held aside seeds 21 through 40 at β 0, 1, 2 and 4, the unchanged demonstration seed and a case with every source corrupted. Lower error can favor dense prediction. The scenario does not establish an advantage for every reliability map or every added attack.

Archived intervention behavior

Their existing continuous target markers retain their original meaning. Replay does not apply the new manual environment retroactively.

Archived settings also retain the original background only reference and the unannounced solar outage. The reference uses the original source map at bus multiples of three. The outage removes solar output during comparison actions 30 through 49 and restores it at 50 without entering forecasts. These scenarios remain available only for archived reproduction. Their complete results remain in the reference audit, outage audit and extended error audit.

Displayed quantities

cₜ = 35‖vₜ − v̄‖² + 0.08‖uₜ‖²

Voltage is in pu and reactive power in kvar. Cost uses the bounded reactive command on the 1 MVA base. The live chart sums executed stage costs. The numerical audit also reports the terminal Riccati penalty separately and includes it in total cost. A physical disturbance changes the next state, whose state cost appears at the following action.

Now and Peak show the largest voltage deviation from each bus’s nominal reference, in mpu where 1 mpu is 0.001 pu. Limited counts clipped inverter commands. The 0.95 to 1.05 pu band is illustrative and is not enforced. Voltage and cost are never clipped. A forecast error in mpu is a forcing error, not an actual voltage excursion.

The shared controller key appears before the plots. Its buttons change curve visibility only, not controllers, measurements or accumulated costs. Every method remains in the cost tables even when its curve is hidden.

Matrix entries show actual allowed prediction sources. Inspecting a cell reveals the last observed forecast error and the source’s contribution to an inverter command. Source, other messages and feedback sum before limiting. History changes only the displayed topology. Playback speed changes viewing pace rather than physical sample time.

LinDistFlow neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. This environment omits unbalanced phases, thermal line limits, protection, frequency dynamics and battery energy limits. The feeder, reliability model and operating profiles are constructed. The paper uses a different coupled model, six discovery episodes and twelve remote sources per receiver.
