Forecasting changing feeder demand

A constructed 24 bus radial feeder supplies homes, solar generation and batteries. The default experiment asks whether useful advance forecasts can keep voltage closer to its nominal profile when some forecast sources are corrupted. It is an illustrative physical scenario, not measured utility data or a numerical reproduction of the paper.

Forecasted operating cycle

The default scenario adds repeating 60 second operating profiles to the existing correlated background power with a 6 kW scale. Household demand varies by ±35 kW around its nominal level. Solar generation falls by 0 to 30 kW below its 80 kW nominal output. Battery power varies by ±15 kW around its nominal demand. These synthetic profiles represent aggregate load schedules, a passing cloud and scheduled charging.

θₜ = 2πt / 60     φⱼ = 2π(j mod 6) / 6
Household power change = 35 sin(θₜ + φⱼ) kW
Solar generation loss = 15(1 − cos θₜ) kW
Battery power change = 15 sin(θₜ + φⱼ) kW

Existing equipment bounds apply after the schedule and background are combined. The displayed scheduled kW describe this known component. Actual power also includes the background and any manual attacks. Every controller faces exactly the same realized power.

These profiles enter forecasts before they occur. A five sample preview includes the current action. The forcing for target action t is sealed at max(0, t − 4) and observed at state t + 1. Previously issued forecasts never change. Only observations already revealed may enter learning. The preview is a declared information assumption, not an implemented commercial forecasting service.

Source reliability

The default assumes reliable inverter forecast sources and corrupted household estimates. All eight inverter sources initially have low error. The fifteen household sources initially have high error. These are synthetic quality classes rather than measured sensor accuracies, and their labels are not supplied to the learner.

A source message estimates voltage forcing at its bus, which depends on power throughout the feeder. It is not simply a forecast of that bus’s own load. Independent Gaussian errors have standard deviation β times a fixed feeder forcing scale, multiplied by 0.05 for quiet sources or 4 for noisy sources. The default β = 4 declares substantial forecast corruption. The physical forcing scale remains based on the original 6 kW background and does not grow with the scheduled profile. No controller gain, objective or actuator limit is retuned.

Clicking a source in Forecast mode corrupts or repairs newly issued messages. Corruption persists until repaired. Marking every source noisy removes the reliable alternatives that communication selection needs. Changing reliability after deployment tests the existing links under different conditions. Rebuild prediction links in Experiment settings starts learning under the current source reliability map.

A fresh comparison

Run collects 180 discovery samples, then starts an independent 180 sample comparison with fresh plant states and costs. The default scenario pauses at the comparison endpoint so the results remain readable. Continue or Step extends that trial. Existing saved runs are preserved. Restart demo clears the entire trial and returns to learning under the selected settings. Experiment settings contains individual steps, playback speed, power request strength and mode, and alternative scenarios. Restore default scenario returns to the default environment.

Learned sparse, Fixed sparse, Dense prediction and Feedback only share the plant, feedback law, realized disturbances, objective and limits. Each sparse controller retains its local source and three remote sources per inverter, totaling 24 remote links. Dense prediction uses 176 remote links. During comparison, selected links and prediction coefficients are fixed. Candidate changes during discovery and deployed connectivity are labeled separately.

The complete scenario audit retains the exploratory grid, all held aside seeds 21 through 40 at β 0, 1, 2 and 4, the unchanged demonstration seed and a case with every source corrupted. Lower error can favor dense prediction. The scenario does not establish an advantage for every reliability map or every added attack.

Unexpected power attacks

Manual power attacks are additional, unforecast stress. Homes increase demand, solar generation decreases and batteries increase charging. Each 50, 100 or 150 kW request decays. Solar loss is bounded by available generation and battery charging is capped at 90 kW. Continuous mode repeats requests at every active bus. Click again to stop one bus or use Stop all. Pause stops repeated requests, while existing disturbances remain in the plant.

All physical costs remain in the same trial. Switching interaction mode or reloading does not erase them. Large repeated attacks can dominate cumulative costs because every controller shares the same feedback. They do not promise to widen the advantage of prediction. The mixed trial label identifies these extra requests.

Experiment settings retain the original background only reference and the unannounced solar outage for reproducibility. The reference uses the original source map at bus multiples of three. The outage removes solar output during comparison actions 30 through 49, restores it at 50 and is absent from forecasts. Their complete results remain in the reference audit, outage audit and extended error audit.

Displayed quantities

cₜ = 35‖vₜ − v̄‖² + 0.08‖uₜ‖²

Voltage is in pu and reactive power in kvar. Cost uses the bounded reactive command on the 1 MVA base. The live chart sums executed stage costs. The numerical audit also reports the terminal Riccati penalty separately and includes it in total cost. A physical disturbance changes the next state, whose state cost appears at the following action.

Now and Peak show the largest voltage deviation from each bus’s nominal reference, in mpu where 1 mpu is 0.001 pu. Limited counts clipped inverter commands. The 0.95 to 1.05 pu band is illustrative and is not enforced. Voltage and cost are never clipped. A forecast error in mpu is a forcing error, not an actual voltage excursion.

Matrix entries show actual allowed prediction sources. Inspecting a cell reveals the last observed forecast error and the source’s contribution to an inverter command. Source, other messages and feedback sum before limiting. History changes only the displayed topology. Playback speed changes viewing pace rather than physical sample time.

LinDistFlow neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. This environment omits unbalanced phases, thermal line limits, protection, frequency dynamics and battery energy limits. The feeder, reliability model and operating profiles are constructed. The paper uses a different coupled model, six discovery episodes and twelve remote sources per receiver.
