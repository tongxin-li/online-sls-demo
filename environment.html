Forecasting changing feeder demand

A choice of constructed radial feeders supplies homes, solar generation and batteries. The default experiment asks whether useful advance forecasts can keep voltage closer to its nominal profile when some forecast sources are corrupted. It is an illustrative physical scenario, not measured utility data or a numerical reproduction of the paper.

Network sizes

The Network menu starts a fresh trial on the chosen feeder. Every size uses the same physical equations, equipment ratings, cost weights and learning rules. Counts and results belong to the selected network. Absolute cost sums over its buses and inverters, so costs from different sizes are not directly comparable.

Forecasted operating cycle

The default scenario adds repeating 60 second operating profiles to the existing correlated background power with a 6 kW scale. Household demand varies by ±35 kW around its nominal level. Solar generation falls by 0 to 30 kW below its 80 kW nominal output. Battery power varies by ±15 kW around its nominal demand. These synthetic profiles represent aggregate load schedules, a passing cloud and scheduled charging.

θₜ = 2πt / 60     φⱼ = 2π(j mod 6) / 6
Household power change = 35 sin(θₜ + φⱼ) kW
Solar generation loss = 15(1 − cos θₜ) kW
Battery power change = 15 sin(θₜ + φⱼ) kW

Existing equipment bounds apply after the schedule and background are combined. The displayed scheduled kW describe this known component. Actual power also includes the background and any manual attacks. Every controller faces exactly the same realized power.

These profiles enter forecasts before they occur. A five sample preview includes the current action. The forcing for target action t is sealed at max(0, t − 4) and observed at state t + 1. Previously issued forecasts never change. Only observations already revealed may enter learning. The preview is a declared information assumption, not an implemented commercial forecasting service.

Source reliability

The default assumes reliable inverter forecast sources and corrupted household estimates. Inverter sources initially have low error and household sources have high error. Their counts are 3 quiet and 4 noisy for the toy, 8 and 15 for the reference, and 16 and 30 for the larger network. These are synthetic quality classes rather than measured sensor accuracies, and their labels are not supplied to the learner.

A source message estimates voltage forcing at its bus, which depends on power throughout the feeder. It is not simply a forecast of that bus’s own load. Independent Gaussian errors have standard deviation β times a fixed feeder forcing scale, multiplied by 0.05 for quiet sources or 4 for noisy sources. The default β = 4 declares substantial forecast corruption. The physical forcing scale remains based on the original 6 kW background and does not grow with the scheduled profile. No controller gain, objective or actuator limit is retuned.

The main trial heading summarizes current β and the configured quiet/noisy source counts. It is not an error measurement, and the learner never receives these labels. The summary changes immediately when a source setting changes, while already issued messages stay sealed. Noise disabled at β 0 does not announce manual power attacks. Clicking a source in Forecast mode corrupts or repairs newly issued messages. Corruption persists until repaired. Marking every source noisy removes the reliable alternatives that communication selection needs. Changing reliability after deployment tests the existing links under different conditions. Rebuild prediction links in Experiment settings starts learning under the current source reliability map.

Ongoing adaptive protocol

Fresh unsaved sessions use one ongoing experiment. Source local calibration continues from revealed forcing and immutable issued forecasts after a 30 pair warmup with a 60 pair window and the existing physical ridge. Its received diagonal correction scales the actual feasible predictive response used by the next executed commands. Plant time, source history and accumulated stage costs never reset at 180 or 360 samples. All methods retain the same stabilizing feedback, physical input, equipment limits and bounded inverter commands.

Adaptive sparse and Fixed sparse use the same offline selected support and mandatory local sources. The former learns source weights and sends an extra correction scalar per admitted forecast. Fixed sparse and Dense prediction retain identity correction. Dense prediction uses its own full remote budget and feedback uses no prediction. Each predictive method uses the documented stationary finite memory response. No online topology selector or probing is implemented, and excluded forecasts do not enter the sparse receiver. Link permission, nonzero gain and numeric payload are different quantities.

The source inspector separates revealed error, received correction, signed current target gain, temporal gain norm, additive source contribution and bounded command. Its gain trace uses immutable executed receipts. The initial inverter inspects its own mandatory source so the main forecast action addresses the displayed weight. Prediction support stays fixed while weights change. This is a declared experimental specialization, not an exact manuscript implementation or a theorem for arbitrary adaptation and clipping.

Actual version 13 trials remain exact archived replays and display an archived heading. Restart demo and Network selection explicitly start a fresh paused ongoing trial. Restore default scenario explicitly replaces the old trial and starts ongoing playback. Until one of those actions, playback, attacks, inspection and cost switching keep the archived algorithm and all old costs. Advanced archived reproduction controls keep their declared finite protocol.

The following finite discovery and comparison lifecycle and recorded results belong to archived runs. They cannot supply performance percentages for the ongoing protocol. Playback pace depends on network size and rendering load while each physical sample always represents one second. Earlier slow 47 bus outcomes are retained in the linked engineering evidence. Retaining all samples through more than 1800 actions is finite evidence, not an indefinite memory guarantee. Exact validation and publication status remains in the repository progress record.

Lower error can favor dense prediction. The scenario does not establish an advantage for every reliability map or every added attack.

Unexpected power attacks

Manual power attacks are additional, unforecast stress. Homes increase demand, solar generation decreases and batteries increase charging. Each 50, 100 or 150 kW request decays. Solar loss is bounded by available generation and battery charging is capped at 90 kW. Continuous mode maintains the selected additional requested kW at each active bus by replacing that bus’s previous continuous component each sample. It does not silently multiply a 150 kW setting through overlapping requests. Applied power still respects equipment bounds. Stopping lets the final request decay with the existing equipment response. Pulse mode remains additive, including when held to repeat; its separate residuals can add to a continuous component. Saved trials recorded before this protocol retain their exact original stacked replay. Click again to stop one bus or use Stop all. Pause stops repeated requests, while existing disturbances remain in the plant.

All physical costs remain in the same trial. Switching interaction mode or reloading does not erase them. Large repeated attacks can dominate cumulative costs because every controller shares the same feedback. They do not promise to widen the advantage of prediction. The mixed trial label identifies these extra requests.

Experiment settings retain the original background only reference and the unannounced solar outage for reproducibility. The reference uses the original source map at bus multiples of three. The outage removes solar output during comparison actions 30 through 49, restores it at 50 and is absent from forecasts. Their complete results remain in the reference audit, outage audit and extended error audit.

Displayed quantities

cₜ = 35‖vₜ − v̄‖² + 0.08‖uₜ‖²

Voltage is in pu and reactive power in kvar. Cost uses the bounded reactive command on the 1 MVA base. The live chart sums executed stage costs. The numerical audit also reports the terminal Riccati penalty separately and includes it in total cost. A physical disturbance changes the next state, whose state cost appears at the following action.

Now and Peak show the largest voltage deviation from each bus’s nominal reference, in mpu where 1 mpu is 0.001 pu. Limited counts clipped inverter commands. The 0.95 to 1.05 pu band is illustrative and is not enforced. Voltage and cost are never clipped. A forecast error in mpu is a forcing error, not an actual voltage excursion.

The shared controller key appears before the plots. Its buttons change curve visibility only, not controllers, measurements or accumulated costs. Every method remains in the cost tables even when its curve is hidden.

Matrix entries show actual allowed prediction sources. Inspecting a cell reveals the last observed forecast error and the source’s contribution to an inverter command. Source, other messages and feedback sum before limiting. History changes only the displayed topology. Playback speed changes viewing pace rather than physical sample time.

LinDistFlow neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. This environment omits unbalanced phases, thermal line limits, protection, frequency dynamics and battery energy limits. The feeder, reliability model and operating profiles are constructed. The paper uses a different coupled model, six discovery episodes and twelve remote sources per receiver.
