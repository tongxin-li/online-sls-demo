A distribution feeder under changing power

The experiment regulates a constructed, balanced radial feeder with 24 buses. A fixed substation supplies homes, solar generation and batteries. The target is the nominal voltage profile before power disturbances.

Disturbances and forecasts

Background net power follows a correlated process with a 6 kW scale. Forecasts are fixed when issued. Every controller receives the same physical disturbances and forecasts.

Communication topology is selected by default. Forecasts equal the resulting voltage forcing plus independent Gaussian errors. Noise level β is chosen from 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2, initially 1. Sources at bus multiples of three have error standard deviation 4β times their physical forcing scale. Other sources use 0.05β. These scales depend only on the feeder and background generator. Quality labels are not supplied to the learner.

In Forecast mode, clicking a bus corrupts or repairs its forecast source. Marked sources remain noisy until repaired. Only newly issued forecasts change. Their noise uses the selected level above. The final source reliability map is retained when comparison starts. The learner sees observations rather than the source quality labels shown on screen.

In Power disturbance mode, clicking homes adds demand, solar reduces generation and batteries increase charging. Requests of 50, 100 or 150 kW add and then decay. Solar loss is limited by available generation, and battery charging is capped at 90 kW. Orange badges show actual applied kW. Continuous mode repeats requests until stopped. Pulse mode adds one per click or repeats while held. These unexpected attacks are absent from forecasts and primarily test the common feedback.

Comparison protocol

One 180 sample discovery episode uses dense prediction messages. Comparison then resets states and costs on an independent disturbance stream. All four controllers share the feeder, limits, objective and disturbances.

Communication topology compares Learned sparse, Fixed sparse, Dense prediction and Feedback only. Each sparse policy receives its local source and three remote sources per inverter. Their coefficients and selected links stay fixed during comparison, isolating the effect of link selection. Sparse policies use 24 remote prediction links and dense uses 176. Eight local channels and common state feedback are excluded.

Changing source reliability during comparison tests the already deployed links in a new environment. Use Learn again to discover links for that environment. Power disturbances test a different mechanism. Without inverter saturation, an unforecast disturbance adds the same state and input response to all four fixed controllers. Its common cost can dominate their prediction differences. Full physical costs remain in every curve.

The complete benchmark reports all five noise levels over twenty paired trials. This feeder uses one discovery episode and three remote sources per receiver. The paper uses six episodes, a different coupled model and twelve remote sources. Its numerical results are not reproduced here.

Prediction adaptation is a separate option with reliable, uniformly corrupted and mixed forecasts. Matrix and scalar corrections continue learning during its comparison under fixed sparse links. Its costs combine adaptation and selection effects.

Displayed quantities

cₜ = 35‖vₜ − v̄‖² + 0.08‖uₜ‖²

Voltage is in pu and inverter injection in kvar. Cost uses the bounded reactive command on the 1 MVA base. Live cumulative cost sums executed stage costs. A completed benchmark adds the terminal voltage error penalty. A disturbance changes voltage at the next sample and is charged then.

The 0.95 to 1.05 pu band is a reference, not an enforced constraint. Voltage and cost are never clipped. Playback speed changes viewing pace without changing the one second physical sample.

Matrix history records actual candidate links during discovery. Added links are green and removed links amber. Comparison uses fixed selected links. Green and amber then compare the learned topology with fixed sparse, while history retains actual discovery changes. The cost gap view subtracts the matching sparse baseline, while the table retains totals.

LinDistFlow neglects line losses and assumes small voltage deviations. This experiment omits unbalanced phases, thermal line limits, protection, frequency dynamics and battery energy limits. It is a constructed environment rather than a measured utility case.
